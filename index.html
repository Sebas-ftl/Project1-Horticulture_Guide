What is Horticulture?

  Horticulture is the practice of growing and caring for plants such as fruits, vegetables, flowers, and shrubs. Do you need to be a farmer or botanist to participate? No! Anyone can follow horticulture practices and growing methods whether for potted plants, lawn plants, or small backyard planter farms.

"horticulture, the branch of plant agriculture dealing with garden crops, generally fruits, vegetables, and ornamental plants. The word is derived from the Latin hortus, “garden,” and colere, “to cultivate.” As a general term, it covers all forms of garden management, but in ordinary use it refers to intensive commercial production. In terms of scale, horticulture falls between domestic gardening and field agriculture, though all forms of cultivation naturally have close links."
- Britannica

Key Terms

Drainage
The natural removal of extra water from a plant. Some plants enjoy consistently moist soil while other prefer the opposite. Its imporant to know what your plant needs as bad drainage can lead your plant to root rot or drying out!
Soil-Mix
Soil is one of the ways we can control drainage as well as provide neutrients. To control drainage we can add clay, sand, coco coir, and rocks. Clay and coco coir are better at retaining water, keeping soil moist / damp whereas sand and rocks assist in keeping water moving out.
Exposure
The level of sunlight a plant experiences on a daily basis. Not all plants enjoy sitting under the sun, some prefer shady places under an awning, and other prefer to be indoors with little to no sun.
Annuals
A plant thats known to complete it's full life cycle in a single year, usually withering away in the cool of Fall & Winter.
Biennial
A plant thats known to complete its complete life cycle in two years insead of one. These plants tend to become dormant during the Winter season and continue its cycle at the return of Spring.
Perennials
A plant thats known to live for more than two years and returns to bloom when the season returns. Evergreen plants, trees, and shrubs fall under this catagory. Its important to note that flowers in this catagory may look dried and brown in the winter but they're actually hibernating underground!
Propagating
Towards the end of a plants lift cycle theres a stage where the plant is ready to bud new life. This varys from plant to plant as some provide seeds, sprout roots at a "node", simply from planting its leafs, etc.
